# Terminology: Knowledge Capitalism vs. Cognitive Capital

Naming rationale for the framework's two core terms, and the editorial rules
that keep them distinct. Settled 2026-07 (session with Remy; recommendation
approved).

## The two terms and their roles

- **Knowledge Capitalism** — the *regime*: the framework's name for the
  economic system in which knowledge is the primary source of value. Used in
  the site title, the framework name, and nowhere as an asset word.
- **Cognitive capital** — the *asset*: the accumulated, compounding capacity
  to act effectively under uncertainty, held in a mind, a model, or an
  institution. Defined in Factor 9. It is a *capability*, not a stock.

The split is deliberate and does conceptual work: the framework is named for
the knowledge economy as a system, while its central asset is deliberately
NOT the knowledge itself but the capacity to activate it. Factor 1 grounds
this (knowledge is defined by consequence), and Factor 9 argues it explicitly
(ideas are nonrival, recombinant, and unpriceable without disclosure — the
warehouse is a poor asset; the capability is what networks can score).

## Editorial rules

1. **"Knowledge capital" is a banned term** in the current corpus. Where the
   stock-sense is needed, write "the stock of knowledge" in plain words.
   (The term appears only in `drafts/` archives, which are historical and
   left untouched.)
2. The one-line definition of cognitive capital lives in Factor 9; everywhere
   the term appears before Factor 9 in reading order (currently: homepage
   In brief), it is a glossary popover with the definition, the
   knowledge-capital contrast, and a link to Factor 9.
3. Never write "knowledge itself becomes the primary form of capital" — the
   precise formulation is "the capacity to activate knowledge becomes the
   primary form of capital." (The imprecise version was fixed 2026-07 in
   `index.astro`, `consts.ts` BLOG description, and the README intro.)
4. Companion coinages stay as they are: **knowledge activation** (the
   objective function, defined in Factor 1), **knowledge liquidity** (what
   mechanisms maximize, Factor 12). Neither is an asset word.
5. **Activation physics** (settled 2026-07): knowledge by itself is inert;
   activation combines knowledge with **compute and energy**. The standard
   prose phrasing is "compute and energy" (not "work"), with thermodynamic
   precision carried by the Factor 1 citations (Szilard 1929, Landauer 1961,
   Schrödinger 1944). Primary exposition lives in Factor 1's mechanism
   section; one-sentence echoes in Factors 5 and 6 and the homepage method
   bullet. The canonical manifesto paragraphs do NOT yet carry this
   amendment — extending the README manifesto text is a separate, pending
   editorial decision of Remy's.

## External-literature collisions (why the names are what they are)

- **"Cognitive Capitalism" as a system name is colonized.** It is
  Moulier-Boutang's term from autonomist-Marxist critical theory (2011), and
  separately the title of Rindermann's controversial IQ-and-national-wealth
  book (2018). Naming the framework "Cognitive Capitalism" would import one
  or both lineages. The framework name therefore stays *Knowledge*
  Capitalism.
- **"Knowledge capital" collides with accounting-flavored literature** — the
  OECD's "knowledge-based capital" program and the 1990s intellectual-capital
  literature (Edvinsson, Sveiby) — which is stock-oriented, exactly the
  reading Factor 9 argues against. Using it as our asset term would invite
  the warehouse reading.
- "Cognitive capital" as an *asset* term has lighter, less committed prior
  usage, and its capability connotation matches the definition.
