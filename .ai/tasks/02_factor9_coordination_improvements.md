## Current State

```markdown
### 9. Coordination Requires Shared Beliefs and Communication Channels

Cognitive actors cannot coordinate through knowledge alone—they require shared belief systems that align expectations and communication channels that maintain alignment. [Douglas Engelbart](https://www.dougengelbart.org/pubs/papers/scanned/Doug_Engelbart-AugmentingHumanIntellect.pdf) understood that augmenting human intellect was fundamentally a coordination problem, requiring tools that helped groups think together. Shared beliefs reduce the overhead of coordination by establishing common ground; communication channels maintain coherence as conditions change. Organizations, markets, and institutions are all mechanisms for sustaining these shared structures.
```

## The Harari Perspective (from Mentor Memo 2)

Key insight: **Shared fictions/myths enable civilization-scale cooperation**

Relevant quotes:
- "Fiction has enabled us not merely to imagine things, but to do so collectively."
- "Such myths give Sapiens the unprecedented ability to cooperate flexibly in large numbers."
- "Any large-scale human cooperation—...—is rooted in common myths that exist only in people's collective imagination."
- "All large-scale cooperation is based on a common story."

This adds the anthropological/evolutionary dimension: shared beliefs aren't just efficiency mechanisms—they're the uniquely human capability that enables coordination at scale impossible for other species.
