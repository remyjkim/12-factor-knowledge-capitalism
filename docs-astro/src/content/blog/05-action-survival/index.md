:::brief
- Nothing a system knows affects its survival until it acts. Action is the only causal chain from knowledge to consequence.
- The agency interface has three working parts: beliefs that settle enough to commit on, frameworks that compress an intractable world, and plans that sequence decisions.
- Real agents are bounded — they satisfice, not optimize — so effective agency is a balance: beliefs stable enough for action, adaptable enough for learning.
:::

## The claim

Action against reality is the only causal chain toward survival. A system can perceive perfectly, model beautifully, and know truly — and none of it touches the world until it *does* something. And doing is physical: the arrow from knowing to acting is a powered arrow, paid for in compute and energy. Agency is the groundwork on which knowledge earns its consequence, and in economic terms it is where value is created or captured: the point where knowing converts into doing.

The conversion runs through the agency interface: the beliefs we hold, the frameworks we reason within, and the action plans we commit to in making sequential decisions. Belief is the linchpin. Peirce defined it as the settled state that guides action — the cessation of doubt that permits commitment.[^1] An agent that cannot settle cannot commit, and an agent that cannot commit cannot act.

## The mechanism

How does an agent actually hold the world in its head well enough to act on it? Not completely. Formally, agents maintain belief states — probability distributions over possible world states, updated through observation and action.[^2] Practically, no real agent computes over the full distribution, because rationality is bounded: cognitive actors satisfice rather than optimize, relying on frameworks that compress an intractable world into actionable models.[^3]

The mind's own architecture shows the compression at work. Deliberate System 2 reasoning produces judgments that, through repetition, compress into System 1's automatic intuitions — yesterday's slow analysis becoming today's fast reflex.[^4] A framework, in this sense, is cached deliberation: the expensive computation done once, amortized over every decision that reuses it.

## Why it matters

Effective agency is a balance the loop must hold: belief systems stable enough for action but adaptable enough for learning. Too rigid, and the agent keeps executing a plan the world has invalidated. Too fluid, and it never commits long enough for consequences to teach it anything.

This is also where the evolution interface cashes out. The conjectures that survived criticism do not act on the world by themselves — they become beliefs and frameworks, and *those* convert into committed plans. Evolution learns; agency acts; and the trajectories of experience that acting generates are the raw material for the next round of learning. Where that conversion physically lives — in what kind of system — is the next factor's subject.
